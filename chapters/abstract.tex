\chapter*{Abstract}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Non-Invasive Monitoring System for Older Adults Based on Computer Vision and Adaptive Behavioral Learning}
\end{center}

In Argentina, more than 7.4 million people are aged 60 or over, and fewer than half live in multigenerational households.
Many spend much of the day alone, exposed to gradual changes in their routine that anticipate a decline in health and to falls that can go unnoticed for hours.
Panic buttons and wearable devices depend on the older adult using them at all times, and they are often abandoned.
Conventional security cameras do not solve the problem either, because they force the family to keep watching, and many send video to the manufacturer's servers, exposing the older adult's personal data.

This project presents Lares, a non-invasive monitoring system that gives families immediate and continuous information about the older adult.
Video from the cameras is processed inside the home, on a Raspberry Pi 5 without a GPU, and only the detected events reach the cloud, never images.
A first module classifies daily activities by combining a vision language model with the movement of the skeleton, and detects falls and prolonged immobility through pose estimation.
A second module builds in the cloud a baseline specific to each resident, drawn from their own history rather than from generic thresholds.
With it, the system flags deviations from the resident's routine.
A mobile application, which several family members of the same household can share, notifies alerts and shows routine reports.

The requirements emerged from a survey of caregivers and from interviews with two family members, who described their experience of care, and with an older woman, who spoke about her routine and the risks that worry her most.
Each module was validated on public datasets covering daily activities recorded in domestic settings, simulated falls and the real routines of older adults' households.

Activity classification reached 82.8\% accuracy across four categories.
The system detected 83.5\% of 158 falls with nine false alerts, none of them in 1.40 hours of continuous home video, and issued each event on average 1.9 seconds after the fall began.
On the real routines of 21 households, the baseline flagged 76.8\% of the injected deviations.
The business model, based on the sale of the kit and a monthly subscription, is profitable in the base scenario, with an internal rate of return of 31.6\%.

The results show that the understanding of daily activities, fall detection and alerts on routine deviations can be combined in a single system that never takes video out of the home.
With Lares, distance is no longer a problem.
